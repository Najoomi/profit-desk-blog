Many Etsy pricing mistakes come from one habit: pricing from your costs and forgetting that Etsy takes several separate fees, each calculated on a different base. This guide goes through every fee, works one full example to the cent, and ends with two formulas you can use with a calculator.

*All fee figures below: fees verified 27 Sep 2026, editable. Etsy changes fees from time to time, so check the linked Help Center pages before you rely on them.*

## The Etsy fees that affect your price

### 1. Listing fee: $0.20

You pay **$0.20 per listing**, whether or not it sells. A listing lasts 4 months. If you list a quantity above 1, Etsy charges **$0.20 again each time one sells** and the listing auto-renews for the remaining stock. If a buyer takes several in one order, you pay $0.20 for each extra quantity. The simplest way to plan is to **treat $0.20 as a per-sale cost.**

### 2. Transaction fee: 6.5% of the order total

Etsy charges **6.5% of the total order amount, including the shipping and gift wrap you charge**. Many sellers miss this: moving part of the price into "shipping" doesn't avoid the fee, because shipping is charged at 6.5% too.

### 3. Payment processing fee: depends on your bank's country

This is charged on every Etsy Payments order, as a percentage plus a fixed amount. The rate depends on **where your bank account is**. A few examples from Etsy's table:

| Bank account location | Processing fee |
|---|---|
| United States | 3% + 0.25 USD |
| United Kingdom | 4% + 0.20 GBP |
| Germany / France (most EU) | 4% + 0.30 EUR |
| Canada (domestic or orders from the US) | 3% + 0.25 CAD |
| Australia (domestic orders) | 3% + 0.25 AUD |
| India | 5% + 25 INR |
| Pakistan | 6.5% + 0.30 USD |

It is calculated on the item's **total sale price including shipping and any applicable sales tax**. It is *in addition to* the 6.5% transaction fee.

### 4. Offsite Ads fee: 15% or 12%, only on ad-attributed orders

If a buyer clicks one of Etsy's ads for your item on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest and so on, and buys from your shop within 30 days, that order carries an advertising fee:

- **15%** of the order total if your shop has always made under $10,000 in any 365-day period. You can opt out.
- **12%** once your shop has made $10,000 or more in a 365-day period. From then on it is mandatory.
- The fee is **capped at $100 per order**.

You can't choose which orders come from ads, so it's sensible to price so that an ad order still isn't a loss.

### 5. Currency conversion: 2.5%

If your **listing currency is different from your bank account's currency**, Etsy converts your earnings and charges **2.5%**. For example, a UK seller who lists in USD and gets paid into a GBP account pays this. You avoid it only if the currencies match.

### Other charges to know about (not in the main US example)

- **One-time shop set-up fee:** non-refundable, "cost varies by location".
- **Regulatory operating fee:** a percentage of the item price plus shipping for sellers in Canada (0.50%), France (1.14%), Hungary (1.97%), Italy (0.80%), India (0.05%), Spain (0.88%), Türkiye (1.67%), the United Kingdom (0.48%) and Vietnam (1.24%). The UK example below includes it.
- **Etsy Ads** (on-site ads you choose to run), **shipping labels**, **deposit fees** in some countries, and **VAT on seller fees** where it applies.

## A fully worked example

A US seller (US bank account) makes a candle.

**Inputs**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Item price | $24.00 |
| Shipping charged to buyer | $5.00 |
| Materials (wax, wick, fragrance, jar, label) | $6.00 |
| Packaging | $0.80 |
| Actual postage paid | $4.50 |
| Labour: 20 minutes at $15/hour | $5.00 |
| **Total costs (C)** | **$16.30** |

**Step 1: order total (R)**
R = $24.00 + $5.00 = **$29.00**

**Step 2: Etsy fees**

| Fee | Maths | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction | 6.5% × $29.00 | $1.885 |
| Payment processing | 3% × $29.00 + $0.25 = $0.87 + $0.25 | $1.120 |
| Listing | flat | $0.200 |
| **Total fees** | | **$3.205** (about $3.21) |

**Step 3: net profit and margin**
Net profit = $29.00 − $3.205 − $16.30 = **$9.495** (about $9.50)
Margin = $9.495 ÷ $29.00 = **32.7%**

Because labour is counted as a cost, this $9.50 is profit *on top of* paying yourself $15/hour.

**Step 4: the same order from an Offsite Ad (15%)**
Ad fee = 15% × $29.00 = $4.35 (well under the $100 cap)
Net profit = $9.495 − $4.35 = **$5.145**. Margin = $5.145 ÷ $29.00 = **17.7%**

Same candle, same price: the ad order earns about half as much.

**Step 5: the same candle sold by a UK seller (UK bank, listing in GBP)**

Same numbers, in pounds: item £24.00, shipping £5.00, costs £16.30, so the order total is £29.00. The listing fee is charged as USD 0.20; at an assumed rate of about 1.33 USD per GBP that's about £0.15 (the real rate varies).

| Fee | Maths | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction | 6.5% × £29.00 | £1.885 |
| Payment processing | 4% × £29.00 + £0.20 = £1.16 + £0.20 | £1.360 |
| Regulatory operating fee (UK) | 0.48% × £29.00 | £0.139 |
| Listing | USD 0.20, about | £0.150 |
| **Total fees** | | **£3.534** (about £3.53) |

Net profit = £29.00 − £3.534 − £16.30 = **£9.166** (about £9.17). Margin = £9.166 ÷ £29.00 = **31.6%**.

**If that UK seller's listing currency differed from their GBP bank account** (for example, listing in USD), add the 2.5% currency conversion. On a £29.00-equivalent order that's about 2.5% × £29.00 = £0.725, so fees ≈ £4.259, net ≈ £8.441 and margin ≈ **29.1%**. *(Etsy applies the 2.5% to the funds it converts. Working it out on the full order total slightly overstates it, which is the cautious direction.)*

Not included here: VAT on Etsy's seller fees, which applies to many UK sellers. Check your own VAT position.

## Two formulas you can use with a calculator

Set up your numbers once:

- **p** = all percentage fees added together, as a decimal. For a US bank with no ad that's 0.065 + 0.03 = **0.095**.
- **F** = fixed fees per order. US: $0.25 processing + $0.20 listing = **$0.45**.
- **C** = your costs per item, including materials, packaging, postage and labour. Here **$16.30**.
- **S** = shipping you charge the buyer. Here **$5.00**.
- **m** = target margin (net profit ÷ order total). Here **0.30** (30%).

Net profit on an order total R is: **R × (1 − p) − F − C**

### Break-even price (zero profit, labour still paid)

> **Order total needed = (F + C) ÷ (1 − p)**
> **Item price = order total − S**

Example: (0.45 + 16.30) ÷ (1 − 0.095) = 16.75 ÷ 0.905 = **$18.51** order total, so the item price is 18.51 − 5.00 = **$13.51**.
Check: fees at $18.51 = 0.095 × 18.51 + 0.45 = $2.21, and 18.51 − 2.21 − 16.30 = $0.00. ✔

### Target-margin price

> **Order total needed = (F + C) ÷ (1 − p − m)**
> **Item price = order total − S**

Example at 30%: 16.75 ÷ (1 − 0.095 − 0.30) = 16.75 ÷ 0.605 = **$27.69** order total, so the item price is 27.69 − 5.00 = **$22.69**.
Check: fees = 0.095 × 27.69 + 0.45 = $3.08; net = 27.69 − 3.08 − 16.30 = $8.31; 8.31 ÷ 27.69 = **30.0%**. ✔

**If you want ad orders to hit the target too**, add the ad rate to p: p = 0.095 + 0.15 = 0.245.
16.75 ÷ (1 − 0.245 − 0.30) = 16.75 ÷ 0.455 = **$36.81** order total, so the item price is **$31.81**. (The ad fee is 0.15 × 36.81 = $5.52, far below the $100 cap. The formula only holds while the ad fee is under the cap.)

**UK bank, listing in GBP:** p = 0.065 + 0.04 + 0.0048 (regulatory fee) = 0.1098, and F = £0.20 processing + about £0.15 listing = £0.35.
30% target: (0.35 + 16.30) ÷ (1 − 0.1098 − 0.30) = 16.65 ÷ 0.5902 = **£28.21** order total, so the item price is **£23.21**.
Check: fees = 0.1098 × 28.21 + 0.35 = £3.45; net = 28.21 − 3.45 − 16.30 = £8.46; 8.46 ÷ 28.21 = **30.0%**. ✔
Listing currency different from your GBP bank? Add 0.025 to p (approximate, in GBP terms): 16.65 ÷ (1 − 0.1348 − 0.30) = 16.65 ÷ 0.5652 = **£29.46** order total, so an item price of about **£24.46**.

**If p + m is 1 or more, no price works.** The fees plus your target already take the whole sale. Lower the target or cut costs.

### What about digital downloads?

Set S = 0 and leave out materials and postage. For a $12.99 printable (US bank):
fees = 0.095 × 12.99 + 0.45 = $1.234 + $0.45 = **$1.684**, net = 12.99 − 1.684 = **$11.31** before your time and tools. With digital products the fees are a small share of the price. The real question is how many sales it takes to pay back the hours you spent making it.

## Common pricing mistakes

1. **Charging fees on the item price only.** The 6.5% transaction fee and the processing fee include shipping (and processing includes sales tax). Always work fees out on the full order total.
2. **Leaving your time out.** If labour isn't a cost, a "profitable" item might pay you $3 an hour. Put your hourly rate into C.
3. **Using someone else's processing rate.** It depends on your bank's country. 3% + $0.25 is the US rate; the UK is 4% + £0.20; most EU countries are 4% + €0.30. Some countries also add a regulatory operating fee.
4. **Forgetting currency conversion.** Listing in USD with a non-USD bank account costs another 2.5%.
5. **Pricing as if Offsite Ads never happen.** At 15%, an ad order can halve your profit (see Step 4). Check your price still works on an ad order.
6. **Adding percentages the wrong way.** A 30% margin does **not** mean "costs × 1.3". Use the formula: divide by (1 − p − m).
7. **Counting the listing fee once.** With quantity above 1, the $0.20 is charged again on each sale that auto-renews the listing.
8. **Never re-checking fees.** Put a date next to your fee rates and re-check them every few months.
